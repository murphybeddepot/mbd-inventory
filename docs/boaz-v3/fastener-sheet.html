Cam & bolt — the joint that builds the cabinet

Cam housing, ∅15

262.26.535 V2 → V3 unchanged
Hole∅15 × 15 deep, face
Centre from mating edge34  (43 on the Top→End joint)
Bolt run into it∅8 × 34 edge bore
Count in V3146
Drive#3 Phillips, half turn

The cam is a steel drum with a spiral slot cut inside it. Dropped into its ∅15 hole, the slot opens toward the panel edge. The bolt's mushroom head arrives down the ∅8 bore and sits in the mouth of that slot. Half a turn clockwise and the spiral closes on the head, dragging the bolt — and the panel it is screwed into — hard against the joint.

That is the whole trick: the cam does not turn the bolt, it swallows it. Which is why the arrow must point at the edge the bolt comes from before you assemble anything.

Wrong way: a cam dropped in with the arrow pointing into the panel has no open mouth facing the bolt. The panel will sit down but nothing will draw up, and forcing the screwdriver rounds the slot.

Connecting bolt, ∅5 thread — 34 reach

262.28.624 V2 → V3 unchanged
Screws into∅5 face hole, 13–15 deep
Reach, face to head centre34
Head∅7 mushroom
Count in V3138
Drive#3 Phillips on the collar

The bolt is a wood screw at one end and a mushroom at the other. The threaded end bites into the bare ∅5 hole; the collar under the head stops it dead at the panel face, which is what sets the 34 mm reach. Get that collar seated and every bolt on the panel stands at exactly the same height — that is what lets a whole end panel drop onto its mate in one go.

Drive it until the collar touches and no further. An over-driven bolt pulls its own thread out of the melamine and the joint never comes tight.

Telling bolt holes from shelf holes: bolt holes sit alone, roughly 10 mm in from an edge or on a panel centreline. Shelf-pin holes come in ladders — five pairs, 32 mm apart. Never put a bolt in a ladder.

V3 moved the Top-to-End cams from 34 mm in to 43 mm in, and deepened the bolt run from 34 to 40. A 34-reach bolt dropped into this joint stops 9 mm short of the cam — the head never enters the spiral, and the cam spins freely with nothing in it.

Stations on both parts match exactly: 1D carries its four bolts at 56 / 184 / 333 / 461 and 2A its four cams at 264 / 392 / 541 / 669, the same 128 / 149 / 128 spacing off each datum. So the joint is real and deliberate — it just needs a bolt the V2 pack does not carry.

Open item: hardware pack 450.81.593 lists one pin only. Either a second, longer pin joins the pack, or these eight cams move back to 34.

Every other bolt in the bed screws into a panel face. These three per side screw into the panel's edge, through the 19 mm core rather than across it, which is why they take a heavier ∅8 thread: edge grain in melamine core holds far less than face.

They make the joint that V2 did not have. In V2 the lower bed end and the upper bed end were held together only by the Bottom Brace screwed across them. V3 joins the two ends to each other directly — three bolts into cams, three dowels between them.

Do not substitute. A ∅5 bolt in a ∅8 edge bore has nothing to bite. This is the one place in the bed where the thread diameter changes.

Dowels carry no clamping force. They sit between the three edge bolts and do one job: hold the two bed ends in line with each other, front to back, while the cams pull them together. Without them the upper end can creep a millimetre or two and the cabinet face stops being flat.

The 16 mm depth is the tell. Dowel bores are 16 deep and bolt bores are 13, on the same edge, alternating. A dowel pushed into a bolt hole stands 3 mm proud and the panels will not close — which is deliberate, and the reason the dimples that used to mark them were deleted.

Open item: the pack carries no dowel line item. Six ∅8×30 dowels per bed need adding, or the joint ships unlocatable.

Shelf support

Shelf support pin, ∅5

282.04.711 V2 → V3 unchanged
Hole∅5 × 13 deep, face
Pins shipped48 — four per shelf
Hole positions in V3240
Ladder pitch32 — five pairs per shelf

The only fastener in the bed that is meant to come back out. It presses into the hole by hand and its collar stops flush against the panel, leaving a ledge the shelf rests on. Nothing clamps — the shelf simply sits on four of them.

The ladders give five heights per shelf. There are 240 hole positions in a Queen and only 48 pins, because 192 of those holes exist so the customer can move a shelf later.

Reading the panel: a bookcase end carries 30 ladder holes and 12 bolt holes. The ladders run in pairs at 57 and 160 from the front edge, in three groups. Anything outside those groups is a bolt hole.

Screws — three lengths, three jobs

All three are Zip-R flat countersunk, #2 square drive, and all three look alike in a tray. What separates them is how much of the shank is left unthreaded, because that is what decides whether a screw clamps two parts together or just holds one.

¾″ #6 — fully threaded

010.22.952 · 105× per bed V2 → V3 unchanged
Length · gauge19.05 · #6 (∅3.5)
Threadfull — no plain shank
Pilot∅3 × 12 face, or none
Wherehinge plates, L-brackets, piano hinges, magnets
V3 pilots16 on 4A / 4B / 4D / 4E

The short one. Every job it does is metal onto wood — a hinge plate, a bracket leg, a hinge leaf. The hardware is already flat against the panel, so there is nothing to pull together and the thread can run the whole length for maximum grip in 19 mm of melamine.

It is also the only screw short enough to go through hardware into a single panel without coming out the other side. Use a longer one on a hinge plate and it will.

Careful near the piano hinge: the hinge line runs past pin holes. Tap the screw point to mark before driving — a ¾″ screw into a bolt hole finds no wood.

1 ¼″ #6 — ¼″ plain shank

The workhorse, and the one whose shape matters most. The top ¼″ carries no thread, so when it passes through the Interior Back the thread never grips the back — it grips only the Top, Deck or Partition on the far side. The head then pulls the back tight against them. A fully threaded screw here would jack the two panels apart instead.

V3 changed both holes it uses. The clearance through the back went ∅5 → ∅3, and the receiving pilot went from 25 deep to ∅3 × 5 — just enough to start the screw straight, shallow enough that it still bites about 14 mm of solid core beyond.

Not pin holes. The small holes down the back of an Interior Back are clearance for these screws. Putting bolts in them is the single most common assembly error on the V2 sheet.

The heavy one, and the only fastener in the bed doing a safety job. It passes through the bracket and 12 mm of drywall and still has more than 25 mm of thread left to bury in framing. The longer plain shank under the head is what lets the bracket pull flat to the wall rather than ride on the thread.

A #8 body in a #6 pilot will snap. These go into studs found with a stud finder and nothing else.

Safety. The cabinet must reach wall framing. Drywall anchors and toggles alone are not an option on a Murphy bed — the failure mode is the whole cabinet coming off the wall. Steel studs, concrete or masonry need their own fasteners, not supplied.

Brackets, hinges and the pivot

Corner brace, 50 × 50 × 15

260.28.905 · 3× per bed V2 → V3 unchanged
Size50 / 50 / 15
Hole spacing25

Into studs6× 1 ¾″ #8, two per stud

Three brackets tie the top of the cabinet back to the wall. One leg takes short screws into the cabinet top, the other takes long screws into framing. They are the entire anchoring system, which is why the long screws matter more than any other fastener on the sheet.

Confirm before proceeding. No part of the bed should be folded or loaded until all three brackets are on studs and the cabinet does not move when pushed.

Piano hinge, 72″

351.09.633 · 2× per bed V2 → V3 unchanged
Length72″ — 1829
Screws¾″ #6, every other hole
Patternzig-zag, offset by one hole
Set down from top3¾″ — 95
Joinseach adjoining pair of bookcases

Each pair of bookcases folds on one continuous hinge. Because the leaves are long and thin, screwing straight down one side then the other bows the hinge and the pair stops folding flat. The zig-zag fixes that: every other hole on each side, offset by one, so the pull alternates across the hinge line and the leaf stays straight.

Start at the centre of one end with two screws, keep the hinge centred as you go, and work outward.

Watch for bolts. The hinge line crosses bolt positions. Tap the screw point to leave a mark first — if the mark lands on a hole, skip to the next.

Bushing bore2 × ∅35 through, on 2A

These are the only bolted, load-carrying joints in the bed — everything else is a knock-down fitting. The bolts pass right through the panel and a nylock nut on the far side takes the load; the nylon collar is what stops a swinging bookcase from slowly backing the nut off. Tighten until the head countersinks into the opposite face.

The ∅15 × 9 sockets in the outer decks are not cams, even though they share the cam diameter. They are the seat the pivot pin drops into, and they line up with the large hole in the black hinge plate.

Reading the deck: an outer deck carries one ∅15×9 socket, four ∅3×12 hinge-plate pilots and four ∅5 clearance holes. Only the ∅15 one is nine deep — every real cam hole in the bed is fifteen.

Before the drawings get made

Six things worth settling first, because each one changes what gets drawn.

  1. The 43 mm reach. Source a longer pin for the eight Top-to-End cams, or move those cams back to 34 and keep one pin in the pack? Drawing a second pin commits the pack to two.
  2. The ∅8-thread bolt. Six per bed, and no item number on file. Which Häfele part is it, so the drawing shows the right head?
  3. The dowels. Six ∅8 × 30 per bed. Confirm the length — 16 into each part leaves 2 mm, which is right, but only if the bores really are 16 on both sides.
  4. Cam count. 140 in the pack, 146 on the file. Bump the pack, or has V3's end joint not been priced yet?
  5. Twelve shallow marks. Every adjustable shelf carries one ∅3 × 3 bore centred on a long edge, and each side toe carries one. Index mark, banding reference, or leftover? They should either be explained on the sheet or removed.
  6. Part identity. V2 keyed every step to the last two digits of a barcode, with a note saying they drift when the CNC re-orders. V3 already has stable part codes on every drawing and label. Key the new sheet to 1A / 3C / 4B instead?
